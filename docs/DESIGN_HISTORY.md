# LATHE landscape fingerprints: design history

**Period:** 2026-11-07 to 2026-11-09, one working conversation (Steve + Claude).
**Scope:** how an open question about loss landscapes for TRUSS became the `lathe_landscape` repo: the reasoning, the decisions, the corrections, and what is still open.

---

## 0. Summary

1. The question started in TRUSS: can the loss landscape itself be a design object, and do "pre-trained loss-scapes" exist? Only in a narrow sense. A landscape is fixed by architecture, data and loss together, so nothing carries over from another problem except things one level up: loss shapes, optimizers, predictors.
2. The defensible version is **bilevel**: learn a surrogate of the outer landscape $M(\phi)$ from logged runs, with strict data tiers so the loop cannot manufacture closure.
3. A run is a **path** on a landscape shared by every seed of a configuration. The caveats to that picture (folds, DisCo batch size, schedules, symmetry) turned out to be design knobs.
4. **[Steve]** placed the work in **LATHE**. LATHE's existing pre-registered kill test already asks the right question. Split: LATHE diagnoses, TRUSS intervenes.
5. **[built]** `lfp`: probes, a training hook, a CLI and a static site. Smoke-tested end to end on a stand-in dataset. Not yet run on ATLAS data.
6. The per-layer conflict scores became a **pinch-point** diagnostic: direction $c$, balance $\rho$, cancellation $\kappa$. A pinch point is a hypothesis about where capacity would help; only a matched-cost intervention test can confirm one.

## 1. Starting point: "various loss landscapes" and pre-trained loss-scapes

**[Steve]** *For TRUSS: what if we use various loss landscapes? Are there such things as pre-trained loss-scapes?* This was framed as deliberately exotic, divergent thinking.

The hard constraint is that a loss landscape is

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta;\, A,\, D,\, \phi)
$$

weights $\theta$, architecture $A$, data $D$, loss design $\phi$. Change any one and you get a different surface. Nothing pre-trained elsewhere describes the MSVtx landscape. Only things one level up can carry over: the loss shape, the optimizer, a predictor over architectures.

Three readings were laid out **[proposed]**:

| Reading | Examples | Status for TRUSS |
|---|---|---|
| Vary $\phi$ (the objective) | Meta-learned losses (ML³, TaylorGLO); differentiable significance (INFERNO, neos) | Training directly on the relative $Z_A$ is the HEP-native divergent branch; not pursued yet |
| Measure the landscape as a metric | Sharpness / Hessian spectrum, mode connectivity, gradient conflict between loss terms | **Adopted**: became the fingerprints |
| "Pre-trained" over architecture space | NAS-Bench tables, performance predictors, zero-cost proxies | Only a surrogate fit on your own runs applies. NAS benchmarks are vision data; zero-cost proxies often fail to beat parameter count |

## 2. Mixing ML with optimization: designing the landscape

**[Steve]** *Mix machine learning with optimization, with the focus on designing a loss landscape. Gather examples generated by training, then design the experiment around them.*

The formal shape is bilevel:

$$
\phi^\star = \arg\max_{\phi}\; M\big(\theta^\star(\phi)\big)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\theta^\star(\phi) = \arg\min_{\theta}\; \mathcal{L}(\theta;\, \phi, A, D)
$$

The inner landscape is over weights; the outer landscape $M(\phi)$ is the external metrics (AUC, %|nc|, ABCD health, $Z_A$) as a function of the loss design. "Gather examples from training" means fitting a surrogate of $M(\phi)$ from logged runs and optimizing over it (multi-objective Bayesian optimization), plus a second surrogate that predicts final metrics from early-training features.

**[Steve]** *Let's not be careless.* The safeguards **[proposed]**:

- **Three data tiers.** Design folds (used freely by the loop), a selection holdout (touched a few times, logged), and a lockbox (touched once). Without the lockbox, a loop that tunes $\phi$ on the closure region manufactures its own closure. This is the same issue as the earlier cross-fitting audit, one level up.
- **Seed noise.** The outer landscape is noisy, and the noise varies from cell to cell. Use replicates and a noise-aware GP.
- **Budget.** Each example is a full training run: tens to low hundreds of points, which is GP territory.
- **Transfer.** A surrogate fit on one set of data/background files describes only that $D$.

## 3. What TRUSS already had

From the TRUSS sessions (plan mk2 §9):

- **Stage 1.** 70 graded runs = 7 cells × 10 seeds (8000–8009), 5-fold CV, one 128-128 architecture. Cells: vanilla BCE; DisCo $\alpha$ = 4, 8, 16, 32; queen $\beta$ = 0.05 and 0.005 at $\alpha$ = 8.
- **Noise floor** (R a8): seed SD AUC1 0.0017, WP %|nc| 2.0, tail %|nc| 3.3.
- **The α front:** WP %|nc| 20.3 / 13.8 / 8.8 / 5.0 at $\alpha$ = 4 / 8 / 16 / 32. Closure improves steadily with $\alpha$; AUC1 costs 0.0024 at 16 and 0.012 at 32.
- **Instability at α = 32:** seed 8005 reached AUC1 0.828 against a cell median of about 0.870, with no collapsed fold. Cell AUC1 SD is 8× the others. This led to the seed-stability guardrail.
- **Stage 2**, set up but not yet reported at the time: 14 specs × $\alpha \in \{16, 8\}$ × 2 seeds = 56 runs. `pinch_probe.py` had produced pre-registered pinch predictions from the Stage 1 checkpoints.

Stage 1's real value is a per-cell noise model, a 1-D front, and checkpoints for probing the inner landscape.

## 4. Landscapes vs runs

**[Steve]** *So for every run there exists a loss landscape?*

Not quite. The landscape belongs to the **configuration** $(A, D, \phi)$. All seeds of a cell share it (per fold, since each fold trains on a different $D$). A **run is a path** across it: the seed chooses the start $\theta_0$ and the minibatch noise. At the outer level, each run is one noisy sample of $M(\phi)$, and the seed SD is the spread of where those paths end. Read this way, the α = 32 seed 8005 is one path that ended in a bad basin of a landscape that high $\alpha$ makes more rugged.

**[Steve]** *The caveats sound like design options.* Sorted by what they change **[proposed]**:

| Caveat | As a knob | Changes | In v1? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Folds / $D$ | Reweighting, curriculum | Landscape | No: it shifts the background distribution the closure depends on |
| DisCo is a batch statistic | $n_{\text{disco}}$, decoupled from the BCE batch | Landscape (bias, smoothness) | Yes |
| Schedules | $\alpha(t) = \alpha_{\max}\min(1, t/\tau)$ | Both | Yes |
| Permutation symmetry | Ensembling / aligned averaging | The deployed model | Analysis only |

Proposed v1 loss-design space: $\phi = (\log\alpha_{\max}, \log\beta, \tau, \log n_{\text{disco}})$. It was reduced to three dimensions for the pilot by dropping $\beta$.

## 5. The experiment, and the move into LATHE

**[Steve]** *Design an experiment to get initial data. The eye is on loss landscapes: a large enough set of them, and ML + optimization to generate better or novel ones. Do we then reverse-engineer the architecture?*

- **The data unit is a fingerprint.** A landscape over about 20k weights can't be stored; a fixed set of measurements at fixed epochs on a fixed probe batch can. Each run becomes a row $(\phi, A, \text{fold}, \text{seed}) \to F_{t_1}, \dots, F_{\text{final}} \to M_{\text{final}}$.
- **Testable thesis:** a fingerprint is a better intermediate representation than the raw settings: it predicts final metrics better than $\phi$, and the relationship carries over across architectures.
- **Reverse-engineering the architecture** is possible as a constrained search, not an inversion. Fingerprints are many-to-one and lose information, and the effects of $A$ and $\phi$ can only be separated if both are varied on purpose.

**[Steve]** *This leans into project LATHE.* LATHE's public plan already had most of the pieces:

| Pilot piece | LATHE equivalent |
|---|---|
| Logging retrofit | Training dump hook |
| Selection holdout | Probe set carved out before CV |
| α axis as a known change | Positive control along the decorrelation sweep |
| "Fingerprints vs $\phi$" | LATHE's kill test: do map coordinates predict held-out closure better than hyperparameters alone? |
| Seed stability | Display only structures that persist across seeds |

**Additions [proposed]:**
- Landscape geometry as a third family of map coordinates (after prediction space and representation), turning the kill test into a four-way contest.
- **Two leakage fixes:** split the probe set into *map events* and *closure events*; and keep final loss values out of the fingerprint features.
- **Ownership:** LATHE measures and maps; TRUSS owns any optimization loop and uses LATHE coordinates only if the kill test passes. Otherwise LATHE would grade its own exam.

**Pilot design [proposed], 102 runs:** 16 Sobol points over $(\log\alpha_{\max}, \tau, \log n_{\text{disco}})$ × 2 architectures × 3 seeds, plus an α = 16 anchor (× 2 architectures × 3 seeds) tying it to Stage 1. Pre-registered analyses:

| ID | Question |
|---|---|
| A1 | Noise |
| A2 | Fingerprints vs $\phi$ |
| A3 | Transfer across architectures |
| A4 | Early prediction |
| A5 | Does the ramp fix instability? |

Kill criteria are written into the plan.

## 6. Fingerprints and the four hypotheses

**[Steve]** *What could we learn in landscape terms? Could we attempt visualizations of the loss fingerprints?*

| | Hypothesis | Probe |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Higher $\alpha$ makes a narrower, rougher basin, explaining the α = 32 instability mechanically | Filter-normalized 1-D slices (Li et al. 2018) |
| H2 | Conflict concentrates in specific layers: pinch-point candidates (**[Steve]**'s term "pinch points") | Per-layer gradient scores (section 9) |
| H3 | A ramp removes an early curvature spike, so timing (not size) of $\alpha$ is the problem | $\lambda_{\max}$, $\lambda_{\min}$ over epochs |
| H4 | A degraded seed sits in a separate basin rather than a badly converged point of the same one | Seed-pair interpolation barrier + prediction distance |

H1, H2 (final epoch) and H4 can run on the Stage 1 checkpoints with no training. H3 needs new runs.

## 7. The repo

**[Steve]** *Build a Python repo + front-end graphs we can build once we run locally. Standalone repo/site. The front end is generated from the files I run locally (ML sig + bg, standard) plus our visualizers.*

**[built]** `lfp`. See the README for layout and workflow.

- **Probes:** per-layer direction / balance / cancellation; Hessian $\lambda_{\max}$ and $\lambda_{\min}$ (shifted power iteration on Hessian-vector products) and trace (Hutchinson); 1-D slices with rise / width / roughness; seed-pair barriers and prediction distance; the minibatch-vs-full dCor gap.
- **Training hook** (`FingerprintRecorder`). It never steps the optimizer and restores the global RNG.
- **CLI:** `probe`, `pairs`, `build-site`, `killtest`. Progress bars and timestamped status lines throughout.
- **Static site:** opens straight from disk, with Plotly and KaTeX from cdnjs. It joins your `ledger.csv` by run name.
- **Adapter contract** (`adapters/TEMPLATE.py`): the only bridge to your training code, with four rules: same probe events, matching weights, exact loss terms, twice-differentiable loss.

**Verification:**
- Analytic tests against known answers.
- Training with and without the recorder gives bit-for-bit identical weights.
- In-training and post-hoc fingerprints agree exactly on the same weights.
- The site renders with no console errors.

**Found while building:**
- **[corrected]** `torch.cdist` inside dCor gives NaN second derivatives on its zero diagonal, which poisoned every Hessian-vector product. Fixed with `abs` pairwise distances. The probes now report a non-finite HVP instead of returning garbage. Your own DisCo code must also be twice differentiable.
- **[corrected]** Power iteration returns the eigenvalue of largest *magnitude*. On the stand-in it turned negative as $\alpha$ grew, so one "top eigenvalue" was mixing sharpness with negative curvature. The probes now report both $\lambda_{\max}$ and $\lambda_{\min}$.

The stand-in panels below come from scikit-learn's breast-cancer table: 4 seeds per cell, a 32-32 MLP, class 0 as "background", "mean area" as the protected variable. They show what the site displays, not physics.

## 8. How big a base run has to be

**[Steve]** *How large would a base run be before we got meaningful things to examine and compare?*

Two rules (two-sided test at 0.05, power 0.8):

$$
n_{\text{seeds/cell}} \approx \frac{2(z_{0.975} + z_{0.8})^2}{d^2} \approx \frac{15.7}{d^2},
\qquad
N_{\text{runs}} \approx \left(\frac{z_{0.975} + z_{0.8}}{\operatorname{artanh} r}\right)^2 + 3
$$

10 seeds per cell detect cell differences of $d \gtrsim 1.25$ seed SDs. Correlations of $r$ = 0.5 / 0.4 / 0.3 need about 29 / 47 / 85 runs. Seed pairs add no independent sample size.

| Tier | What | Training |
|---|---|---|
| A | Stage 1 checkpoints, α = 8, 16 (and 32 if saved), 10 seeds, **fold 0 only** (with CV no event is held out from every fold). Seed 8005 at α = 32 is the built-in check: if no fingerprint singles it out, stop. | None |
| B | **30 runs**: $\alpha \in \{8, 16, 32\}$ × 10 seeds × fold 0, recorder at epochs 1, 5, 10, 20 and final. First check that it reproduces the Stage 1 ledger. | Yes |
| C | The 102-run LATHE pilot, only after B shows usable seed SDs | Yes |

Details: [BASE_RUN_PLAN.md](BASE_RUN_PLAN.md).

## 9. Fighting vs aligned, and pinch points

**[Steve]** *What would a fighting vs aligned score mean?*

The first site showed only the cosine:

![First version of the H2 panel (cosine only)](img/h2_cosine_first_version.png)

$c_\ell = \cos(\nabla_\ell \mathrm{BCE}, \nabla_\ell \mathrm{DisCo})$ says whether the two terms want to move layer $\ell$ the same way ($+1$), independently ($0$) or in opposition ($-1$).

**[corrected]** The first caption, "a layer that stays orange is a pinch-point candidate", overstated it, for two reasons:

1. **Equilibrium identity.** At a stationary point of the total loss, $\nabla\mathrm{BCE} = -\alpha\nabla\mathrm{DisCo}$, so $c = -1$ by construction. A converged model on the trade-off *looks* maximally fighting.
2. **Magnitude blindness.** A cosine of $-0.9$ doesn't matter if one pull is $100\times$ the other.

In the screenshot, the deep orange at α = 0 is the pull DisCo *would* exert if switched on. With zero weight, nothing is actually fighting.

**[built]** Two more scores, now in the probes and on the site:

$$
\rho_\ell = \frac{\alpha\lVert\nabla_\ell\mathrm{DisCo}\rVert}{\lVert\nabla_\ell\mathrm{BCE}\rVert},
\qquad
\kappa_\ell = \frac{\lVert\nabla_\ell\mathrm{BCE} + \alpha\nabla_\ell\mathrm{DisCo}\rVert}{\lVert\nabla_\ell\mathrm{BCE}\rVert + \alpha\lVert\nabla_\ell\mathrm{DisCo}\rVert},
\qquad
\kappa_\ell^2 = \frac{1 + \rho_\ell^2 + 2\rho_\ell c_\ell}{(1 + \rho_\ell)^2}
$$

A stall ($\kappa \to 0$) needs both $c \to -1$ and $\rho \to 1$. **Provisional pinch-point criterion:** $\kappa_\ell < 0.3$ and $1/3 < \rho_\ell < 3$ *during training* (epochs 5–20), in most seeds, more than the other layers.

![H2 panel with direction, balance and cancellation](img/site_h2_pinch_scores.png)

On the stand-in, nothing stalls: $\kappa \ge 0.53$ everywhere, and DisCo usually dominates ($\rho$ up to 20 at α = 16). α = 0 reads $\kappa = 1$, $\rho = 0$.

**[Steve]** *Pinch points are bad? Would these be areas we could optimize in the next phase?*

- **Not necessarily bad.** A pinch is bad if relieving it pushes the AUC–closure front outward (a capacity limit). It is harmless or useful if it is simply where decorrelation has to cost something. Relieving it can even backfire: extra capacity can satisfy minibatch DisCo while dependence survives in a form minibatch dCor misses. A pinch point is a hypothesis about where capacity would help.
- **Yes, they are next-phase targets.** LATHE diagnoses, TRUSS intervenes:

| Pattern | Likely cause | Next-phase fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stalled at every epoch, most seeds | Capacity | Architecture (TRUSS): widen, skip connection, split classifier/decorrelation paths, GRU-style gates |
| Stalled early, clears later | Timing | Loss schedule ($\alpha$ ramp, $\tau$) |
| Stalled only in the final column | Equilibrium | Nothing |

**The deciding test [proposed]:** widening the predicted pinch layer must move the front more than widening a non-pinch layer with the same added parameter count, and more than widening a random layer. Score it on TRUSS's own card: AUC1, WP and tail %|nc|, $Z_A$, health gate, seed stability. Add a mechanism check ($\kappa$ at the targeted layer should rise during training) and a kill criterion (if targeted changes don't beat the matched controls, drop the diagnostic from TRUSS).

Once Stage 2 has run, this can be checked against `pinch_probe.py` with no new training: do $\kappa$ and $\rho$ point at the same layers, and did the specs that relieved those layers move the front?

## 10. From pinch points to a richer landscape

**[Steve]** *Rebuild the site with an info page on the validity of the pinch-point analysis and how it could lead to a richer loss landscape; include the site image.*

**[built]** `lfp/site/info.html`.

![Info page](img/site_info_page.png)

**Validity in brief:**
- **What the scores have going for them:** they don't change if hidden units are reordered; every run uses the same probe events (checked by hash); they use your exact training objective; they're cheap.
- **Ten threats, each with what handles it:** the equilibrium identity, magnitude blindness, probe batch vs training batch, minibatch DisCo bias, Adam rescaling the gradients ($\kappa$ measures cancellation of the gradient Adam receives, not the step it takes), layer granularity, seed noise, arbitrary thresholds, α = 0 runs, and correlation vs cause.
- **Verdict:** the scores are valid *descriptions* of where the two objectives compete on held-out events, and *hypotheses* about where the architecture limits the trade-off. Only an intervention turns a hypothesis into a finding.

**"Richer" landscape** means the trade-off frontier can be reached without stalls, with wider and better-connected basins at a given closure and seeds that land in equivalent places. Pinch analysis is the first instrument that says *where* the current landscape falls short, layer by layer and epoch by epoch.

**The design loop:** fingerprint → pinch hypothesis → intervention (architecture or loss) → re-measure fingerprint and metrics → surrogate, only if the LATHE kill test passes. Each intervention makes a two-part prediction:
- **Mechanism:** $\kappa$ rises at the flagged layer during training; $\lambda_{\min}$ becomes less negative; barriers and seed SD shrink.
- **Outcome:** the front moves.

Measuring both separates "the landscape changed as intended" from "the metric moved for another reason".
